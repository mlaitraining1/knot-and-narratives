The sangeet number the bride swore she'd never agree to

Bengaluru · Three-Day Wedding · Photography + Choreography

Six weeks before the wedding, Ritika sent us a voice note that started with "absolutely not" and ended, forty seconds later, with "okay fine, but nothing where I have to lift my arms above my head."

That one sentence became the entire choreography brief. No overhead lifts, no formations that required her to face away from Devansh's side of the family for more than eight counts, and — her words — "nothing that looks like I rehearsed it, even though I obviously rehearsed it." It's a more common request than you'd think. Most brides don't want to look like performers. They want to look like themselves, just slightly more coordinated than usual.

We built the number around a walking step she already used on the dance floor at other people's weddings — nothing new to learn, just refined and given a formation. Three rehearsals, each under forty minutes, the last one on the actual sangeet stage two nights before. That's the part people skip and shouldn't: rehearsing in the real space changes everything, from where the light hits to how far apart everyone actually needs to stand.

"Every photo of that number is in focus, in frame, on time — because the photographer already knew exactly where I'd be standing."

On the night, it took ninety seconds. Ritika didn't raise her arms above shoulder height once. Her mother-in-law cried, mostly from relief that nobody dropped anyone. The photographs from those ninety seconds are, by a wide margin, the most requested reprint from the entire wedding.
